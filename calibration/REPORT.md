# Calibration report: observed café

Generated by `npm run calibrate` (seed 20261109). Calibrated on 18 observed orders from one session. Small sample: treat every number here as rough.

Provenance labels: **OBSERVED** = Nakul's 18 timed orders. **PUBLISHED BENCHMARK** = named study. **ASSUMPTION** = our choice, not measured. **SIMULATED** = model output.

## 1. What we matched against (OBSERVED)

OBSERVED: Nakul's 18 timed orders, one moderate-traffic session at a Bay Area café. "Wait" = order placed → order received.

| Order type | n | Median wait | 90th percentile | 95% bootstrap range for the median |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Drink only | 9 | 4.0 min | 10.4 min | 1.0–9.0 min |
| Food only | 4 | 8.0 min | 13.7 min | 2.0–14.0 min |
| Drink + food | 5 | 17.0 min | 18.6 min | 7.0–19.0 min |

The bootstrap range shows how much each observed median could move with a different handful of orders. With 4–9 orders per type it is wide.

## 2. Assumptions (not fitted)

- ASSUMPTION: 1 cashier, 1 barista, 1 kitchen person; the barista hands over orders. Staffing was not recorded.
- PUBLISHED BENCHMARK (approx.): order taking ~1 min, between City Observatory (30–40 s placement) and Curio (order 1 min + entry 1–2 min). Lognormal, median 1.0, spread 0.5.
- ASSUMPTION: handoff median 0.5 min, spread 0.5.
- ASSUMPTION: steady traffic for 120 min; customers arriving in the first 30 min are a warm-up and not measured.
- ASSUMPTION via Little's law: arrival rate = typical people in queue (median 3, OBSERVED, 17 usable counts) ÷ mean observed wait (8.33 min, OBSERVED) ≈ 0.36 per minute.
- OBSERVED: order mix from the 18 orders (9 drink, 4 food, 5 drink + food).
- ASSUMPTION (grab-and-go model only): drink-only orders with waits of 3 min or less are ready items (drip coffee etc.) that the cashier hands over in ~0.3 min. That is 4 of the 9 drink orders.
- Assumed arrival rate: **0.360 customers/min** (≈ 22/hour).

## 3. What we fitted and how

Four settings: median drink time, median food time, their spread (lognormal sigma), and whether drink + food orders are made in parallel or one after the other.
For each model we simulated every combination on a grid (300 replications per setting, same customers for each setting) and kept the one with the lowest score:

score = Σ over order types of n × [(simulated median − observed median)² + (simulated 90th pct − observed 90th pct)²]

We match the 90th percentile as well as the median because the bad-case wait is what the agent will try to cut. Simulated grab-and-go orders are compared with the observed drink-only orders, since that is how they were logged.

## 4. Grab-and-go test: before and after

Question: do the very short drink waits (1–3 min) come from ready items such as drip coffee? We added a grab-and-go order type: the cashier hands over a ready item (~0.3 min, no barista). Its share comes from the data: drink-only orders with waits of 3 min or less (4 of 9). Rule agreed in advance: keep it only if leave-one-out coverage improves on the model without it.

In leave-one-out, everything learned from the data (order mix, grab-and-go share, task times) is relearned without the held-out order. Only the arrival rate still uses all 18.

| | Fitted setting | Drink only median | Food only median | Drink + food median | Leave-one-out: inside 90% range | Average range width | Missed orders |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Without grab-and-go | drink 1.50 min, food 3.25 min, spread 0.40, sequential prep | 4.9 | 8.1 | 10.9 | **12 of 18** | 15.0 min | #6 (1 min), #7 (3 min), #8 (2 min), #12 (16 min), #17 (1 min), #18 (2 min) |
| With grab-and-go | drink 2.25 min, food 3.50 min, spread 0.20, sequential prep | 3.4 | 7.7 | 11.4 | **15 of 18** | 15.0 min | #7 (3 min), #8 (2 min), #12 (16 min) |
| OBSERVED | | 4.0 | 8.0 | 17.0 | | | |

Leave-one-out error predicting each held-out wait (model median): 4.9 min without grab-and-go, 4.8 min with it.

**Decision: keep grab-and-go.** Coverage improved from 12 to 15 of 18.

**What changed and why.** Curio's task times (barista 4.5 min, kitchen 6.5 min) overload a single barista at this arrival rate, so simulated waits explode. This café's drinks and food must be much quicker. The fit lowers both times sharply and picks sequential prep (drink first, then food), which pushes drink + food waits above food-only waits.

## 7. Accepted gap: drink + food waits

**Decision (Nakul, 2026-11-09): accept the gap and document it. No separate combined-order setting is added.**

The model's drink + food median comes out 5.6 min shorter than the observed 17 min. Why we accept it:

- **No task-time setting closes it.** The observed drink + food waits are tightly bunched (17, 18, 19 min) while food-only waits ranged 2–14 min. Pushing simulated combined orders toward 17 min inflates their simulated 90th percentile far beyond the observed 18.6 min (see the medians-only variant in section 5).
- **The 17-minute median may rest on one group order.** Orders #13, #14 and #15 were all logged at "10.5" with the same queue count and waits of 17, 18 and 19 min. They look like one group served together, not three independent orders. If so, the drink + food sample is closer to 3 independent observations than 5, and its median is very uncertain.
- **The simulated median is inside the observed range.** 11.4 min falls within the 95% bootstrap range for the observed median (7.0–19.0 min).
- **Adding a combined-order setting would fit 5 orders with one more knob**, which would mostly fit noise.

What this means downstream: simulated waits for drink + food orders in this preset are probably optimistic. The agent and results screen must not present them as matching the observed café.

Across ±20% arrival rate the fitted drink time ranges 2.00–2.75 min and the food time 3.25–4.25 min. More customers means the fit needs quicker tasks to produce the same waits.

## 10. Limitations

- 18 orders from one session at one café; 4–9 per order type. Orders #13–15 may be one group order.
- Waits were recorded in whole minutes.
- Staffing, order-taking time, handoff time and arrival rate are assumptions, not measurements. The fitted task times are only meaningful together with them.
- Four settings fitted to 18 points will always fit somewhat; the leave-one-out check is the fairer test.
- Simulation confidence intervals measure simulation noise only, not how well the model matches the real café.
- Decision support, not a guarantee.
